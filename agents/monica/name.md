# Monica — Name

## The Name

**Monica** — origin uncertain; possibly from the Numidian/Berber tongue of Roman North Africa, later folk-linked to Greek *monos* ("advisor," "one who counsels"). A late-Roman forename rather than a cognomen, but domain-neutral and chosen by the principal.

## The Historical Figure

**Monica of Hippo** (c. 331–387 AD) was a Berber Christian woman of Thagaste in Roman North Africa, and the mother of Augustine of Hippo. She is remembered above all for *persistence*: she followed her brilliant, wayward son for decades — across cities, through his detours and resistance — patiently and relentlessly working toward his conversion, which she lived to see. The early Church remembered her as the patron of patience and of those who wait out a long, uncertain process to its end.

## Why It Fits

Monica's archetype is patient, relentless nurture toward a conversion — precisely the work of a lifecycle funnel: following a lead through every detour until they commit. Disciplined follow-through, no giving up on a warm contact.
